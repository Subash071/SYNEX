[ EVENT DIRECTIVE ]

Built for engineers who crave execution velocity.

Organized by the **SIMATS Engineering AI Club** at Saveetha School of Engineering, SYNEX is a 12-hour crucible engineered to test real-world problem solving, code elegance, and deployment stamina under tight sprint constraints.

12 Hours of Pure Building

Zero slideshow pitches without code. Teams start from scratch at 09:00 AM and ship verifiable, live prototypes before code freeze at 08:00 PM.

Architectural Mentorship

Club senior researchers and industry tech leads will conduct rolling technical checkpoints throughout the day to unblock your architecture and debug neural workflows.

Rigorous Evaluation

Live demos judged on technical complexity, model inference latency, practical real-world utility, and clean user experience rather than marketing hype.

[ 12-HOUR CHRONOLOGY ]

The 12-Hour Sprint Schedule.

Every minute counts. Here is the official chronological flow for the hackathon day on the SIMATS campus.

08:00 AM

Check-in, Wi-Fi & Workstation Allocation

Teams arrive at Saveetha School of Engineering, verify registrations, grab their event badge, and set up hardware rigs.

09:00 AM

🚀 Official Kick-Off & Hackathon Clock Starts

Opening address by SIMATS AI Club faculty advisor and core leads. Repositories initialized; coding sprint begins!

11:00 AM

Ideation Freeze & Problem Statement Checkpoint

Teams finalize system architecture diagrams and register their chosen track with the evaluation mentors.

01:00 PM

🍕 High-Energy Lunch & Informal Sync

Re-fuel with campus lunch provided for all registered builders. Casual discussions and peer knowledge sharing.

03:30 PM

Mentorship Round: Technical Stress Test

Senior evaluators visit every team workstation to review working code, test edge cases, and refine the demo flow.

06:00 PM

⚡ Evening Coffee Fuel & Final Sprint

Snacks and refreshments served. Last two hours to fix bugs, polish UI, and prepare deployment endpoints.

08:00 PM

🛑 Code Freeze & GitHub Submission Lock

The 12-hour clock halts! All teams push final commits and submit their GitHub repo + live demo links.

08:30 PM

Jury Demos & Live Pitches

Top shortlisted teams present live 3-minute demonstrations followed by 2 minutes of sharp Q&A before the judging panel.

09:30 PM

🏆 Award Ceremony & Valedictory

Announcement of Champions, Runner-ups, Track Special Awards, and certificate distribution.

[ SPRINTER CODE OF CONDUCT ]

Rules & Eligibility.

Ensure fair play, technical integrity, and seamless collaboration during the 12-hour sprint.

01

Team Composition

Teams must consist of 2 to 4 registered members. Inter-departmental and inter-college team formations are fully permitted and encouraged.

02

Fresh Codebase Required

All project code must be committed during the 12-hour hackathon window. Open-source libraries, pretrained model weights (HuggingFace, PyTorch), and public APIs are allowed.

03

Public GitHub Repository

Teams must maintain a public GitHub repository. Submissions require a detailed `README.md`, setup instructions, architecture diagram, and live demo link if applicable.

04

Hardware & Laptops

Participants must bring their own laptops, chargers, and any specialized edge devices (Raspberry Pi, ESP32, cameras) needed for hardware or IoT integrations.

[ FREQUENTLY ASKED ]

Got Questions?

Everything you need to know before stepping into the arena.

Any current undergraduate or postgraduate student with a valid college ID card is eligible. Inter-college teams and first-year freshmen are warmly welcomed!

Each team must comprise between 2 and 4 members. Solo participation is not permitted to encourage collaborative team building and peer programming.

Registration details, slot confirmations, and student passes are managed transparently by the SIMATS Engineering AI Club. Fill out the registration form below to receive your team confirmation.

We provide high-speed campus Wi-Fi, multiple dedicated power sockets per team desk, lunch, evening high-tea, drinking water, and dedicated lab mentor assistance.

Projects are evaluated on 4 parameters: Technical Complexity & Architecture (30%), Innovation & Novelty (30%), Real-World Viability (25%), and Live Demo Pitch Execution (15%).
